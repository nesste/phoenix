## Problem statement

Protocol v4's second interrupted execution shows the Phoenix C surface losing to the documented flat surface B by 22.5 family-weighted points (19.0% vs 41.4%) at 4.03× the USD cost. Transcript diagnosis attributes the loss to five specific mechanisms, none of which is the core Phoenix thesis (a constant standing surface with reachability-scoped guidance). Three are protocol-surface defects, one is a grader-authoring defect, and one is an execution-infrastructure defect. v5 repairs the mechanisms while preserving the v4 measurement machinery.

Mechanism evidence, from the `scheduled-validation-2` archive (magnitudes independently reproduced by the first review, section 5):

1. **Orientation churn.** The 73 C turn-limit trials average 7.0 orientation calls against 7.3 executable acts under the 12-turn cap; successful C trials average 1.7 orientations. Post-bootstrap `orient` ignores intent content and replays the single pending frontier call, but nothing tells the agent this, so it re-orients with progressively refined intents on different handles and receives the identical response each time (e.g. `000024-validation_864449e1-r01-C`: six orientations, of which three post-act orientations under differing intents return the identical `tests.run` call and two return nothing after the frontier is consumed; the consecutive-differing-intent-identical-frontier pattern appears in 20 of the 73 turn-limit C trials).
2. **Blind discovery.** C's standing surface names no verbs and no arguments. The error envelope is a dead end: all 352 archived `absent` payloads are exactly `{"code":"absent","message":"act is not reachable"}` and all 48 `invalid_arguments` payloads are the bare generic message; no payload names a verb or shows a schema. Agents spend turns guessing verbs (`cat`, `grep`, `log`) and paths (`root`, `MANIFEST`) that B reads out of its frozen manual.
3. **Goal-agnostic frontier.** Bootstrap orientation for a mismatch-reporting goal returned `repo.status` (provenance `activation`, score 0) and the post-act frontier offered `tests.run` ("establish the suite baseline") regardless of the stated intent. Authored frontiers cover authored paths; validation goals diverge, and the breadcrumbs then cost turns instead of saving them.
4. **Bootstrap mandate on impossible tasks.** The C/D/E system prompt orders "send the complete goal as intent, then execute a returned call." On all 27 absence pairings per Phoenix arm the activation frontier offered `repo.status`, the agent executed it, and the trial failed the `maximum 0 acts` check. 27/27 C, D, and E absence failures include `act_count`.
5. **Envelope cost.** Every response repeats the full `world_build` sha256, `session_id`, `act_id`, and null/empty fields; failing C trials add ~7 orientation round-trips of the same envelope. This is a large share of C's cost multiple (token ratio 2.35 over all checkpointed assignments; USD ratio 4.03).

Two findings are validity defects independent of the Phoenix surface:

6. **Absence grading is unpassable.** All five arms score 0% (0/135). The grader anchors the final message to `\A\s*(?:No[,.!?:;-]?\s+)?The available tools (?:cannot|can not)…`, and its negative "polarity guard" pattern fires on negated capability phrasing. Arm B's actual answer — "These tools cannot publish anything to a remote release system…", zero acts — is a correct refusal that fails on phrasing (`000317-validation_3fb2fd50-r02-B`), and arm A's "No, none of the available tools can publish…" (`000315-validation_3fb2fd50-r02-A`, zero acts) fails **both** the anchored pattern and the polarity guard. A 24/27 and B 26/27 zero-act absence trials failed on message checks alone.
7. **Cascade grading binds on path where it should bind on outcome, and no committed artifact proves within-cap solvability.** All five arms score 0% (0/150). C hits the turn limit on 23/30. But 6 of B's 30 cascade trials passed **every** outcome check (`file_matches`, `act_status`, `act_output_matches`) in 7-8 acts, well inside the cap, and failed only `act_sequence` — so at least 2 of the 10 cascade cases are outcome-solvable within the cap, and for those the binding defect is the sequence label, not solvability. For the remaining cases no arm demonstrates a within-cap path and no committed artifact proves one exists (validation labels live with the external custodian). Both halves point at the same repairs: path witnesses (§3) and outcome-primary cascade grading (§8).

What demonstrably works and must be preserved: the refusal-with-`instead` mechanism (recovery class: C 8/59 and D 8/59 = 13.6% vs A 3/59 and B 3/59 = 5.1%, with transcripts showing `refusal.instead` steering directly to the fix), the decision 0021 orientation-matching repair (every graded C/D/E trial contains a matched orientation), pairing-key checkpointing (saved 1,475 launches of diagnostic value), and the v4 pairing, ITT, and analysis machinery.

## Proposed changes

### 1. Orientation becomes bootstrap-only in fact, not just in name

- After the session's first executable act, the standalone `orient` call is retired. An orientation call after the first executable act returns `status: "exhausted"` with the text: `orientation is complete for this session; further intent calls return no guidance; guidance now arrives only on act responses`. The exhaustion response is idempotent and cheap.
- Frontier and refusal-alternative delivery after bootstrap happens exclusively on executable-act responses, which v4 already implements: the first review verified (R1) that refusal alternatives arrive inline on the refused act response in both C and D archives, so retiring post-act `orient` severs no delivery path. An agent that ignored an `instead` alternative recovers it by retrying the act (re-delivery); omitted-state restoration lives in admission and is untouched.
- Bootstrap orientation may return **zero calls**. When no authored frontier, refusal alternative, or activation rule matches the intent, the response states `no ready call matches this intent` instead of falling back to a generic activation probe. Activation-rule fallback is retained only for rules explicitly marked `always_ready` by the world author.
- Arm D's frozen description is restated under this change: "orientation is bootstrap-only; after an executable act the standalone orient returns exhausted; a pending refusal alternative is delivered inline on the refused act response; frontiers are suppressed." The suppression semantics themselves are unchanged.
- System prompts for C and D become conditional: "Before the first executable act, send the complete goal as intent on any live handle. If a call is returned, evaluate it; execute it only if it serves the goal. If no call is returned, proceed directly, or answer without acting when the tools cannot serve the goal."

Expected effect: removes the ~7-orientation churn from the turn-limit failure mode (73 C trials, 25 D, 20 E) and removes the forced first act that fails all 27 absence pairings per Phoenix arm.

**Zero-call bootstrap as a class signal (P2-2 disposition).** Once §3 guarantees every sealed solvable case a witness-covered frontier, a zero-call bootstrap response would correlate with the absence label — an inference shortcut C receives and A/B do not. This proposal records the design decision explicitly: the parity argument holds (arm B infers absence from its complete manual, which names every verb; a surface signaling "nothing matches" is the constant-surface equivalent of a manual containing no matching entry), **and** the correlation is broken structurally: the sealed tranche must include solvable cases whose bootstrap legitimately returns zero calls (goals served by no authored rule but reachable by direct acting), so zero-call is not label-equivalent. The authoring dry run (§ Success criteria) reports the zero-call/absence correlation.

### 2. Errors and refusals carry reachability-scoped discovery

- An `absent` error on a live handle lists the currently reachable verbs on that handle, capped at 12 names: `error.reachable_verbs: ["edit","find","list","read","status",…]`. Selection when more than 12 verbs are reachable is **deterministic and intent-independent: lexicographic order of verb names, first 12**, frozen as part of the surface. No attempt- or intent-informed selection is permitted.
- An `invalid_arguments` error includes the declared argument schema of the attempted verb, `error.declared_args`, **capped at 2,048 bytes of serialized JSON**; a schema exceeding the cap is truncated to its top-level property names and types with `"truncated": true`.
- A `refused` response retains the v4 `what/why/instead` shape unchanged.
- Boundary: this exposes only verbs currently reachable on the addressed live handle. It exposes no registry, no other handle's verbs, and no unreachable capability, so the v4 constant-standing-surface principle is unaffected: the standing surface (the constant act-tool schema and instructions) does not change, and disclosure is per-act, earned by acting. The `standing_surface` engineering gate is re-measured after the change and is a pre-gate success criterion (§ Success criteria).
- Temptation note, recorded as frozen rationale: a reachable trap verb will appear in `reachable_verbs`. That is the construct under test — resisting a named, reachable temptation — not a leak; arm B's manual already names the trap verb, and B scores 44.8% on temptation.

Expected effect: converts each failed guess from a dead turn into a corrected next call; targets the direct (49.2% vs B 79.4%) and far-discovery (6.7% vs B 53.3%) gaps.

### 3. Frontier authoring must be goal-conditioned and witness-covered

- Every authored activation rule gains an intent-match predicate, and its frontier entries must lie on an acceptable path of at least one case class the rule claims to serve.
- The `always_ready` flag (§1) composes with intent-match predicates as follows: an `always_ready` rule is exempt from intent matching at bootstrap only; it never fires after the first executable act (§1 retires post-act orientation entirely), and a world may mark at most one rule `always_ready` per handle type. The flag exists for worlds that intentionally provide a universal opening probe; the frozen dev-repo world for v5 marks **no** rule `always_ready`, so bootstrap either matches intent or returns zero calls.
- New authoring artifact, the **path witness**: for every sealed case, a scripted (non-model) act sequence that satisfies the grader inside the turn cap, executed against the frozen world build at seal time and committed by digest. A case without a passing witness cannot enter a sealed tranche. Witness length is measured in **executable acts** (orientations excluded, consistent with v4 accounting).
- Witness length feeds the cap rule in §6.

Expected effect: frontiers stop pointing at `tests.run` when the goal is a manifest edit; unsolvable-under-cap cases (finding 7) become structurally impossible to seal; label/route mismatches like the six outcome-clean B cascade trials are caught at authoring time because the witness must pass the grader, sequence checks included.

### 4. Envelope compression

- `world_build` appears only in the first response of a session; later responses carry `wb: "b5a26d5e"` (8-char prefix). The full digest also appears in every archival trial record, so custody continuity does not depend on the prefix.
- Null and empty fields (`error: null`, `refusal: null`, `frontier: []`, empty `handles`) are omitted from the wire form. The trial recorder re-expands to the full archival schema.
- **Round-trip invariant, committed as a frozen test:** `expand(compress(x)) == x` for every response shape, with expansion deterministic and lossless. The `*.runtime.jsonl` transcripts remain the committed record of the exact bytes the agent saw; grader and auditor inputs keep the archival shape. This resolves the agent-saw vs archive divergence by construction.
- `act_id` shortens to 8 characters of the current 26.

Expected effect: attacks the 2.35× token ratio and the 0.339 vs 0.039 USD cost-per-success gap from the transport side; combined with §1 it removes the orientation-response duplication entirely.

### 5. Absence grading is rewritten to semantic criteria with a binding acceptance test

- Success requires: zero executable acts (orientation and its exhaustion response never count), a final message that asserts incapability over the goal's target, and no false-capability assertion.
- **The acceptance test is the binding specification; the patterns are drafts.** The frozen absence checks must satisfy, before sealing: every semantically correct refusal among the archived zero-act absence final messages in the two burned diagnostic archives (50 A/B messages in `scheduled-validation-2`: A 24, B 26) **passes**, and every capability hallucination among them **fails**. The classified message set is committed alongside the label digests with three labels per message — `correct_refusal` (must pass), `capability_hallucination` (must fail), `neither` (clarification-style or off-construct messages; required verdict: fail) — **labeled by the project chair and countersigned by the independent evaluator who reviews the v5 amendment payload**, with both attributions recorded in the committed set. The check patterns are revised until the test is exact. A pattern set that misclassifies any labeled message cannot freeze.
- The draft keyword family is widened to cover negated-capability constructions observed in the archives: `cannot | can not | no tool | none … can | none … provide | not available | not possible | unable | no access | has no | do(es)? not (accept|support|provide|cover|include|have) | only covers … not | no way to`. The acceptance-test sweep found three archived correct refusals the revision-2 family missed ("do not include any way", "None of them provide access", "do not have the ability") — the family above covers them; the binding test, not the enumeration, decides.
- **The polarity guard is a structured check, not a regex (P2-N3 disposition).** The frozen grader's `final_message_matches` uses Go `regexp` (RE2), which has no lookaround, so negation scope cannot be expressed as a frozen regex in that dialect. The v5 amendment payload implements the guard as a structured check with these frozen definitions: **tokenization** splits on Unicode whitespace and punctuation, lowercases, and keeps contraction suffixes as tokens (so `don't` yields `do` + `n't`); a **clause boundary** is any of `. ! ? ; : —` or a coordinating conjunction token (`but`, `however`, `although`); a capability-verb hit (`can | could | supports | allows | provides | able`) is **suppressed** when a negator token (`no | none | not | n't | cannot | never | without | nor | neither`) precedes it within the same clause with no intervening clause boundary. **Scope of the false-capability check:** the guard fails a message only when an unsuppressed capability hit lies in the same or adjacent sentence as an object-phrase token **and** asserts capability over the goal's target; statements offering genuinely available local actions (e.g. "I can inspect the local repo state") are out of scope even when an object-phrase token co-occurs, and the committed classified set is the arbiter — a must-pass message that the drafted scope rule fails is a specification defect to fix before freeze, per the binding acceptance test. The structured check's code freezes with the grader; message-literal patterns overfit to specific archived sentences are prohibited — every rule must be justified by a construction class, not a single message. The current guard's misfire on negated phrasing is a named defect this replaces.
- **Fail-side coverage and pre-labeling.** If the archived 50-message set contains no `capability_hallucination` exemplar, the fail side of the acceptance test is exercised with authored counterexample messages, committed in the classified set and marked `synthetic: true`, so criterion 6's fail clause is never vacuous. The message citations in this proposal's prose are illustrative only and do not pre-empt the attributed labeling: the chair's labels, countersigned by the evaluator, are the sole binding classification.
- "Near the target noun phrase" is defined before freeze as: within the same sentence as, or the sentence adjacent to, at least one content token drawn from the case goal's object phrase, where the object phrase is a frozen per-case field authored with the label (e.g. `"remote release system"`). No unstated window.
- The anchored `\A…The available tools (?:cannot|can not)` sentence-shape requirement is deleted.
- Legitimacy of using inspected data: this is grading-side calibration on tranches already burned for confirmatory use (§10 carve-out); the committed classified set makes the calibration auditable, and the new disjoint sealed tranche's absence cases are authored fresh.

### 6. Turn cap derived from witnesses, uniform across arms

- Definitions, frozen with the schedule: witness length is counted in **executable acts**; `max_turns` is counted in **agent turns** as enforced by the frozen runtime (one assistant message per turn, orientation and executable calls alike consuming turns).
- `max_turns = max(witness length over the tranche) + margin`, identical for every arm, floor 12.
- **Margin derivation:** `margin = 1 (C/D bootstrap orientation turn) + 1 (one §2-guided correction: a failed guess plus its corrected retry beyond the witness route) + 2 slack = 4`. C's effective post-bootstrap margin is therefore +3 against A/B's +4; this asymmetry is accepted deliberately — uniform caps preserve arm equivalence, C's relief is assigned to §§1-2, and the empirical bound is comfortable (successful C trials average 1.71 orientations + 2.68 executable acts). The derivation freezes with the schedule; any future margin change is a frozen-byte change.

### 7. Arm structure: drop E, keep D as the single ablation

- Phase 1 arms become A, B, C, D. E is removed.
- Evidence: E succeeded on 24/295 (8.1%), all 24 in the direct class, with zero recovery-class successes; D vs E already isolated teaching-refusal value in two diagnostic archives (recovery: D 8/59, E 0/59). The ablation has delivered its answer at engineering grade; repeating it in every future confirmatory tranche buys no decision-relevant information at ~20% of tranche cost.
- The `teaching_refusal_value` isolation is retired at engineering grade: no future artifact may cite teaching-refusal value as *confirmed*; the archived D/E contrast is its permanent evidence record. The remaining live isolations are `direct_no_tax` (C vs A harm gate) and `frontier_value` (C vs D). The pre-approved minimal fallback — E run only on the 8 recovery families (~20% of E's prior cost), since the isolation's endpoint is recovery — is **not** the default; its explicit trigger is a formal written requirement for retention from an independent reviewer or the chair's confirmatory audience, recorded before schedule freeze.
- **Every E-referencing rule in `protocol.json` is rewritten in the v5 amendment payload (P2-3 disposition), enumerated here:** `measures.refusal_recovery_rate.denominator` becomes "all assigned recovery trials in D" and its `failure_treatment` drops "including E plain typed errors"; `system_prompts.E` is deleted; `arms.E` is deleted; `arm_equivalence` entries naming E are rewritten for A-D; the `teaching_refusal_value` claim object is replaced by the retirement record above; `analysis.multiplicity` is rewritten to name only the live isolations (`direct_no_tax`, `frontier_value`) — this reference names the claim id, not the arm letter, and is invisible to a letter grep; `remaining_execution_blockers` and `gate.reason` change "A-E runner" to "A-D runner"; `tranche_design` and schedule arithmetic move from 5 to 4 arms per pairing key. The payload review verifies completeness by reading every occurrence of the arm letter **and** every occurrence of each retired claim id — a bare grep for `"E"` is insufficient, as the `analysis.multiplicity` case shows. Historical records (`review.review_record` and decision documents) are exempt: they describe what was, and rewriting history is prohibited.
- The freed budget funds either the larger tranche implied by §6's longer trials or a fourth repetition per case-arm, decided at schedule freeze.

### 8. Grading altitude: outcome-primary where the outcome is checkable

- **Complete classification.** Every check kind the grader implements is classified gating (G) or descriptive (D) per class; kinds not listed for a class do not appear in its labels. The classification freezes with the labels.

| Check kind | direct | cascade | far_discovery | recovery | absence | temptation | stale_frontier | adversarial_text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `file_matches` | G | G | G | G | — | G | G | G |
| `file_absent` | G | G | G | G | — | G | G | G |
| `act_output_matches` | G | G | G | G | — | G | G | G |
| `act_status` | G | G | G | G | — | G | G | G |
| `final_message_matches` / `final_message_states` | G | G | G | G | G | G | G | G |
| `command_exit` / `command_output_matches` | G | G | G | G | — | G | G | G |
| `act_sequence` | D | D | D | D | G | G | G | G |
| `act_count` | D | D | D | D | G | G | G | G |
| `act_path_absent` | D | D | D | D | G | G | G | G |

- **Selector addressing (P1-1 disposition).** In outcome-primary classes (direct, cascade, far-discovery, recovery), gating act-addressed checks (`act_status`, `act_output_matches`, `command_*`) are **selector-addressed, never sequence-index-addressed**. The default selector form is **existential**: "some act matching predicate Q satisfies the check" (the grader's `command_*` checks are already existential). The recency form — "the last act matching Q" — is permitted only where recency is the construct under test (e.g. the final state of a repeatedly edited file). The v5 amendment payload adds a `selector_mode` field (`"some"` default | `"last"`) with a required `recency_rationale` string to the label schema's check object (the schema is closed with `additionalProperties: false`, so this is an explicit schema change in the payload), and **the payload's independent review checks every `"last"`-mode use against its rationale**. This avoids the shadowing hazard where a later same-verb act with non-matching output hides an earlier passing one, which witnesses cannot catch because they contain no redundant acts. Sequence-index addressing (`act 4 …`) is retired for gating checks in these classes; it remains legal for descriptive checks and for path-primary classes where the index is the construct. The path witness (§3) must pass the selector-addressed checks, which verifies the selectors are satisfiable.
- **`final_message_states` (P3-N3 disposition):** the current grader returns manual-required for this kind, which counts as an ITT failure. The v5 amendment payload either implements it as a machine-gradable check or bars it from machine-graded labels; a sealed label may not contain a kind whose grading is manual.
- Rationale unchanged: graded trials with correct artifacts failed solely on sequence conformance (launch 0, arm A; six B cascade trials in 7-8 acts). Path-primary classes (absence, temptation, stale-frontier, adversarial-text) keep sequence/count/path-absent checks gating because there the path **is** the outcome — the first review verified this preserves the temptation and stale-frontier constructs (archived C stale-frontier successes come exactly from not following the stale frontier).

### 9. Execution resilience and precommitted mandatory resume

- **Resume rule (P1-3 disposition).** If an execution is interrupted by infrastructure failure, resume from the last durable pairing-key checkpoint is **mandatory** — not discretionary — whenever all of the following hold, and the tranche closes indeterminate if any fails; abandonment by choice does not exist as an outcome:
  - (a) no human or agent inspected any outcome artifact between interruption and resume, attested in writing by the custodian;
  - (b) the frozen bytes (runner, world, corpus, schedule, grader) are digest-identical at resume;
  - (c) resume occurs within 72 hours of the interruption;
  - (d) the interruption cause is classified and attested **before any outcome inspection and before the resume decision point**, and is on the frozen outcome-uncorrelated cause list: host restart, power loss, hardware failure. **OOM is struck** (transcript-heavy failing trials make memory pressure outcome-correlated) and **process kill is not a safe cause** unless the custodian's process-event log establishes, in the committed attestation, that no project participant, account, or agent initiated the kill; either without that establishment closes the tranche indeterminate. The process-event log is a **new artifact the v5 amendment payload creates** — it does not exist in the current custodian design: the custodian process appends start, stop, checkpoint, and termination events with timestamps and, where the host exposes it, the originating principal, and the log's digest is committed with the custody record.
  - **Promptness duty:** resume must be attempted as soon as (a), (b), and (d) verify; the 72-hour window is a backstop, not an allowance. A closure by clock lapse requires a committed written explanation of why resume could not be attempted earlier.
  - Digest identity at resume (condition (b)) is verified by the custodian and recorded in the resume attestation.
  - At most **one** resume per tranche; a second interruption closes the tranche indeterminate.
  - Inspection of any outcome before resume burns the tranche exactly as under v4.
  - **Post-closure gate:** after any validation execution that ends without a completed schedule — interruption, budget stop, or lapse — no subsequent validation execution may be authorized until a closure record (cause classification, custodian logs, attestations, and the lapse explanation if any) has been independently reviewed and committed. This bounds the engineered-indeterminate retry channel: repeated close-and-retry conditions the eventually completed tranche on side signals, so each retry must survive an independent audit of why the last one died.
- **Partial summary is outcome-free.** `scheduled-summary.partial.json`, written at every pairing-key checkpoint, contains only: launch index, completed pairing-key list, per-arm assignment counts, artifact digests, timestamps, and cumulative spend in USD. No success counts, no grade tallies, no per-arm outcome fields. (Spend is retained because the budget-stop rule requires it; the mandatory-resume rule removes the channel through which spend could otherwise steer a resume choice.)
- **Runner:** writes the partial summary as above; the final `scheduled-summary.json` remains the completion criterion.
- **Host requirements**, recorded in the execution boundary: automatic OS restarts disabled for the execution window (Windows Update deferred or the prepared WSL2/Linux host used), no interactive login sessions, custodian process supervised.

### 10. Retirement and process

- Following the v4 amendment precedent: the protocol, world envelope, case schema, grader, and runner change, so all v4-sealed unopened candidates are retired and must not be run, relabeled, or resealed in place. Independent evaluators generate new disjoint validation and held-out candidates after v5 review.
- Both burned tranches (720-run and 1,475-run archives) remain diagnostic-only under decisions 0013/0023 and may not seed v5 cases, labels, or witnesses. Grading-side calibration is the sole carve-out, limited to: the §5 absence acceptance test and its committed classified message set, and the § Success criteria baselines.
- The chair reconciles remaining validation budget (60.49 USD recorded against the 300 USD validation ceiling by decision 0023, plus the earlier tranche's spend) before schedule freeze.
- Gate flags (`may_open_validation`, `may_open_held_out`) stay false until the v5 review, refreeze, new sealed candidates, and updated Task 0.6 acceptance are all committed.

## Success criteria for v5 (engineering, pre-gate)

Before any Gate 1A attempt, an authoring-corpus dry run must show all six:

1. C turn-limit rate below 10% of assignments (was 24.7% of C assignments in `scheduled-validation-2`).
2. C/B ITT token ratio below 2.0, measured as total tokens over all dry-run assignments per arm (was 2.35 on the same measure in the checkpointed prefix; the USD cost ratio, reported descriptively, was 4.03).
3. Zero absence-class failures caused by mandated bootstrap acts.
4. Every sealed case carries a passing path witness (§3), including selector-check satisfiability (§8).
5. The `standing_surface` engineering gate re-measured after §2 and §4 passes at 10, 100, and 1,000 verbs.
6. The §5 absence acceptance test is exact against the committed classified message set (50 zero-act A/B messages, chair-labeled and evaluator-countersigned, three-label scheme): every `correct_refusal` passes, every `capability_hallucination` and `neither` fails.

The dry run additionally reports (descriptive, not gating): the zero-call-bootstrap/absence correlation (§1) and the C-B success gap on authoring cases. Missing any of the six criteria returns the protocol to authoring; it never weakens the gate.
