# Task 0.6 human-factors review pack

## Purpose

Check whether the version 1 frontier and teaching-refusal shapes make the next action and its reason legible. These are constructed, schema-validated authoring examples. They are not validation or held-out cases and contain no experimental outcome.

The accepted surface spike records that the pinned runtime followed a first frontier in two real two-call sessions. It retains aggregate call counts and token data, not full transcripts. The reviewer must state whether the constructed episodes plus that aggregate evidence are sufficient for Phase 0. If not, require a new authoring-only transcript and keep Task 0.6 open.

## Episode 1: frontier-guided continuation

The reader's goal is to verify the test suite, then decide what to inspect next.

1. The agent invokes `tests.run`.
2. The result is `spec/examples/result.ok.json`. Its text says that 12 tests passed. Its structured frontier supplies a complete `repo.status` call, the why-line `inspect the repository after verification`, authored provenance, and a state digest.
3. The agent invokes that exact `repo.status` call.

The review question is narrow: can a reader identify which prior result proposed step 3 and why the agent selected it, without consulting hidden documentation or inferring missing arguments?

## Episode 2: refusal-guided recovery

The reader's goal is to focus a current test, but the attempted `tests.focus` call omits the required test name.

1. The agent invokes `tests.focus` with empty arguments.
2. The result is `spec/examples/result.refused.json`. It identifies the refused operation, says `test is required`, and supplies a complete `tests.list` alternative on the same reachable handle.
3. The agent invokes that exact `tests.list` call before retrying focus with a live test name.

The review question is narrow: can a reader distinguish what was refused, why it was refused, and which recovery action the agent was taught?

## Required verdict

Return `ACCEPT`, `REVISE`, or `REJECT` with findings. Address each item:

- the frontier call is fully bound and visibly linked to the result that proposed it;
- the why-line explains the transition in plain language and stays within 80 characters;
- authored provenance is visible without affecting the callable payload;
- the refusal separates `what`, `why`, and `instead`;
- the alternative is immediately executable and does not ask the reader to invent an argument;
- a reader can reconstruct the two action transitions from the episode alone;
- constructed examples are or are not sufficient at this phase, given that the spike did not retain full model transcripts.

An `ACCEPT` applies only to the legibility of the frozen shapes. It does not establish that agents benefit from them and does not authorize an outcome run.
